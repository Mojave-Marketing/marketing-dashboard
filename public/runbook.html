Regular Posts

Target is 2–3 posts per week. The flow is: draft content → sync with Christy to gut-check messaging and tone → pair with a Canva visual (templates created by either the marketing coordinator or Heather) → publish. Christy is not a gatekeeper for every word — she's a second set of eyes to make sure the post makes sense and lands well.

Cadence: 2–3x/week Visual: Canva template per post Review: Christy before publishing

Opportunity

Recent Context

Transitioning

During a period of reduced availability, the approach shifted to reusing high-performing content rather than creating net new. This was intentional — good content resurfaces well on LinkedIn. As capacity returns, the goal is to move back to original content at full cadence.

Content Pillars

💡 Thought Leadership

Industry trends, energy efficiency, HVAC innovation, IAQ, and sustainability. This is where Mojave earns credibility with engineers, facility managers, and building owners. From sales, there's a push to develop more content around basis of design — link to be shared. This pillar is the core of the LinkedIn strategy.

🔧 Product

ArctiDry product content — technical differentiators, ISMRE, dew point control, liquid desiccant, and how the system works. Demo units and installs belong here. Pull from the "Units in the Wild" Teams channel (RSMs and Justin, Director of Operations post field images) for authentic product-in-use content.

🏗️ Basis of Design

New — build out

Engineering-specific content targeting specifiers, MEP consultants, and engineers. Technical depth on how and why ArctiDry is designed the way it is — ASHRAE compliance, ISMRE standards, dew point math, spec-level detail. A distinct audience and voice from applications content. Direction from sales. Link to basis of design resource to be shared.

🏥 Applications

Vertical-specific, outcome-focused content for facility managers, building owners, and decision makers. Healthcare, grocery, education, hospitality. "Here's what ArctiDry does in your type of building." Lunch & Learn events and install posts fit naturally here.

📅 Events

Trade shows, Lunch & Learns, factory tours, and partner announcements. Rep firm welcome posts have performed well here. The "Units in the Wild" Teams channel (RSMs and Justin, Director of Operations post images) is a content source for install and field shots that can feed this pillar.

🙌 Tried & Retired (for now)

Employee spotlights and holiday posts have been tested. Neither has been a consistent priority. Worth revisiting if bandwidth allows, but not a focus pillar right now.

Content Formula

Hook / Value / Story / CTA

150–300 words per post. Open with a hook that stops the scroll, deliver the value clearly, add context or story, close with a CTA. Every post should include a Canva visual with alt-text. Posts without visuals underperform.

Rep firms are their own beast — not employees, not a simple contact list. This tab covers onboarding, contact management, website maintenance, the marketing partners network, and the lead capture process.

Onboarding

How It Works

When a new rep firm is signed, the RSM makes an introduction via email between their key contact and Mojave marketing. This introduction is how we coordinate and get approval from their end before publishing the LinkedIn announcement post. From there, onboarding involves several moving parts across marketing, CRM, and the website.

Contact Import

New rep firm contacts come in a mix of formats — spreadsheets, PDFs, or CSV files. In most cases, the marketing coordinator manually imports these into Zoho CRM. Long-term, this should live with the RSM. The Zoho → Mailchimp automation was set up to handle this automatically but kept breaking — it is currently being reconnected. See Tools tab (Zapier) for status.

When adding rep contacts to Zoho, three fields are critical and must always be set:

  • Mailchimp Segment — required for the Zapier automation to route contacts correctly
  • Language Preference — English or Spanish; Mojave supports LATAM contacts
  • Contact Type — Sales or Marketing; determines how they're communicated to
⚠️ Do not skip these three fields. They are the foundation of how contacts flow into Mailchimp and get segmented correctly.

PDF Management & Redirects

When a PDF Changes

When a PDF is updated, three things need to happen in sequence:

  • Upload the new PDF under Media in WP Admin — this is what generates the new URL for the file
  • Swap the URL on the relevant rep resources page (U.S. or LATAM) in WP Admin to point to the new file
  • Set up a redirect in WP Engine from the old PDF URL to the new one — this ensures any buttons or links elsewhere on the site that point to the old file automatically forward to the updated version
⚠️ Don't skip the WP Engine redirect step. Links to PDFs live in multiple places across the site — missing a redirect means broken downloads for anyone who has the old URL bookmarked or shared.

Lead Scoring & Lifecycle

Current State

No formal lead scoring or lifecycle tracking exists today. Contacts enter Zoho and move forward based on RSM activity, not a defined scoring model. This is a meaningful gap for an HVAC manufacturer where the sales cycle is long and stakeholder types vary significantly.

Proposed Framework

To be defined

Fit signals — who they are:

  • Contact type: engineer/specifier > facility manager > distributor > unknown
  • Vertical: healthcare, grocery, education score highest (strongest ArctiDry fit)
  • Territory: covered by an active RSM

Intent signals — what they've done:

  • Attended a Lunch & Learn or demo unit event
  • Downloaded a spec sheet or guide
  • Filled out the rep locator form
  • Opened or clicked email campaigns
  • Requested a demo unit

Proposed lifecycle stages:

  • Cold → Engaged → MQL → Handed to RSM → Active Opportunity → Customer
⚠️ The infrastructure challenge: fit and intent signals currently live in separate tools — Mailchimp has email behavior, the website has form fills, Zoho has contact records. Nothing connects them today. Lead scoring requires solving that data unification problem first.

Data Flow

How data moves across the Mojave marketing stack — from contact sources through Zoho, into Mailchimp, Power BI, and the website.

Overview

How to Read This

Every contact that enters the Mojave marketing ecosystem passes through the marketing coordinator first. Nothing reaches Mailchimp, Power BI, or the website without being verified and formatted upstream. The diagram below maps the full flow.

Flow Diagram
Automated Manual / human in loop Broken / opportunity
People Tools Sources Outputs
Process start Drag any node to rearrange

Key Principles

Marketing Coordinator is the Single Gatekeeper

All contact data — from events, business cards, rep firm CSVs, or website forms — flows through the marketing coordinator before entering any system. The three fields that must always be set: Mailchimp segment, language preference, and contact type.

Two Parallel Pipelines

Zoho feeds two separate downstream systems that don't talk to each other directly. Mailchimp receives contact and campaign data via Zapier (and manual handoff in the interim). Power BI receives sales data via the Postman pipeline Mateo runs twice a week.

Automation is Partial, Not Complete

The Zapier Zoho → Mailchimp automation exists but is not fully live. Until it is, contacts are also passed to Heather manually. Do not assume automation is handling everything — confirm with the marketing coordinator before making changes to either flow.
